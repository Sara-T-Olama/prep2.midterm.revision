🟢 Slide 2 • Number Systems

The Real Number Family

The Rule & Exam Trick
Rational (\(\mathbb{Q}\)): fractions/decimals. Irrational (\(\mathbb{Q}'\)): non-perfect roots, \(\pi\).
🚨 Trick: A square root is only rational if the number inside is a perfect square!
🏋️ Quick Practice: Classify \(-\sqrt{81}\) as Rational or Irrational.
Explanation: First simplify the radical \(\sqrt{81} = 9\), so \(-\sqrt{81} = -9\). Since \(-9 = \frac{-9}{1}\), it is a Rational Number.
Check inside the radical first!

🟢 Slide 3 • Inequalities & Intervals

Intervals & The Number Line

The Rule & Exam Trick
\([a, b]\) closed, \((a, b)\) open.
🚨 Trick: Infinity always gets a round bracket! You can never touch infinity. Example: \((-\infty, 5]\).
🏋️ Quick Practice: Write the interval for: "All real numbers greater than or equal to \(-5\)."
Explanation: "Greater than or equal to \(-5\)" includes \(-5\) (closed bracket `[`), and extends infinitely towards positive infinity (always round parenthesis `)`). Thus, \([-5, \infty)\).
Infinity always wears a round hat!

🟢 Slide 4 • Radicals

Radicals & Roots (The Golden Rule)

The Rule & Exam Trick
Only add/subtract similar roots.
🚨 Trick: You CANNOT split a root over addition/subtraction! \(\sqrt{a + b} \neq \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}\).
🏋️ Quick Practice: Simplify \(\sqrt{16 + 9}\)
Explanation: Add inside first: \(16 + 9 = 25\). Then take the square root: \(\sqrt{25} = 5\). (\(\sqrt{16} + \sqrt{9} = 4 + 3 = 7\) is a trap!).
Add inside first! Don't split roots!

🟢 Slide 5 • Exponent Laws

Exponents (The Negative Trap)

The Rule & Exam Trick
\(a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}\).
🚨 Trick: Watch brackets! \((-4)^2 = 16\) vs \(-4^2 = -16\).
🏋️ Quick Practice: Evaluate \(2^{-3}\)
Explanation: Negative exponents flip the base: \(2^{-3} = \frac{1}{2^3} = \frac{1}{8}\).
Negative exponent flips the base!

🟢 Slide 6 • Factoring

Factoring Part 1 (GCF & Difference of Squares)

The Rule & Exam Trick
\(a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)\).
🚨 Trick: Always factor GCF first! \(2x^2 - 32 = 2(x^2 - 16) = 2(x - 4)(x + 4)\).
🏋️ Quick Practice: Factor \(x^2 - 121\)
Explanation: Difference of squares with \(a = x\) and \(b = 11\). Formula \((a - b)(a + b)\) yields \((x - 11)(x + 11)\).
Always check for GCF first!

🟢 Slide 7 • Factoring

Factoring Part 2 (Trinomials & Perfect Squares)

The Rule & Exam Trick
\(x^2 + bx + c = (x + m)(x + n)\) where \(m \times n = c\) and \(m + n = b\).
🏋️ Quick Practice: Factor \(x^2 + 9x + 20\)
Explanation: Find two numbers multiplying to \(20\) and adding to \(9\). Those are \(4\) and \(5\) (\(4 \times 5 = 20\), \(4 + 5 = 9\)). Thus \((x + 4)(x + 5)\).
Find factors adding to 9, multiplying to 20!

🟢 Slide 8 • Factoring

Factoring Part 3 (Sum & Difference of Cubes)

S.O.A.P. Rule
\(a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)\).
🚨 Trick: Same sign, Opposite sign, Always Positive!
🏋️ Quick Practice: Factor \(x^3 - 125\)
Explanation: Using difference of cubes formula with \(a = x\) and \(b = 5\) gives \((x - 5)(x^2 + 5x + 25)\) following S.O.A.P.
Remember S.O.A.P. for cubes!
